## 10-017 — Verse on death, memory, and the generations that follow

The first manuscript page of this spread is largely unreadable. It mentions hills, wreaths of flowers, midnight, and thoughts of things that might have been.

The facing page is more readable. These scenes and thoughts awaken us, and we forget the Indigenous person’s grave and the white person’s tomb. They lived and loved, knew joys and follies, yet death broke the cherished bonds that held them to life. The draft then speaks of idle mourning over their revered remains and of desecrating tombs raised by affection to honor and guard the dead. Part of the revised wording remains unclear.

Soon we will pass away, leaving [[unreadable wording]] behind to tell what we were: a single trace. Like fragile spider silk carried by the wind, we will go, and no one may ever mark where we fall. This busy race will have no time to pause when we are gone. The young and cheerful will laugh on, led by life’s [[unreadable wording]]. The final revised line cannot be read securely.

## 10-022 — “We are growing old together Jim”

We are growing old together, Jim.
The years come swiftly on.
Our hair is growing gray and thin;
The days of our youth are gone.
Some thirty years have taken flight
Since you and I were born.
We have gathered flowers, fair and bright,
And felt the piercing thorn.
Where is our gentle [[Mary?]] now,
Who accepted our early [[pledge?]]?
What sorrows [[might she know?]]?
What cares may surround us both?
Perhaps beside a rippling stream,
Where murmuring waters flow,
They live a [[peaceful?]] life
That we may never know.
On the facing page:
[[The opening line is unclear.]]
With all our boyhood’s games,
The bat and ball—but never mind,
Their names are not forgotten.
The sunny scenes of long ago
Are hidden by clouds and gloom,
And many a winter’s icy [[blast?]]
Has nipped the summer’s bloom.
We cannot follow where they have gone;
Our paths have separated widely.
But in the Great Beyond
Nothing can divide us.
The final stanza recalls former companions and says that some have fallen and some sleep, but several words are too faint to read.

Reading note: This is a modern reading of an unsigned poem, retaining uncertain passages. It is not assumed to describe George’s own life or a relative named Jim. See 10-023 for the ending.

## 10-023 — “Enough—we too must go”: the ending of the poem to Jim

Our golden dreams of a better time
Have gone with the shining past.
Those rapturous visions of [[youth?]]
Were all too bright to last.
Why we remain to tell the story,
We may not ask to know.
Enough—we feel life’s piercing pains.
Enough—we too must go.

Reading note: Lightly modernized from the final two stanzas of the poem on 10-022.

## 10-045 — “Parody”: a rose offered in friendship, April 1868

The first rose of summer,
fresh, blooming, and beautiful,
sends its sweet fragrance
into the gentle air.
How sweetly it blushes,
like a young woman’s cheek
when she feels emotions
her lips cannot express.

I will not leave you, beautiful rose,
to bloom on your stem.
I will gather you gently,
the day’s sweetest jewel,
while the dewdrop still rests
on your bright leaves,
like a tear on the eyelashes
when a loving heart grieves.

Soon I will give you
as a token of true friendship
to someone who will value you
with the same affection throughout life.
True hearts never wither,
even when youth’s beauty has gone;
love remains unchanged
where friendship first appeared.

April 1868.

## 10-047 — “Bachelor’s Lament,” July 1875

Oh, where is the hope of a vine-covered cottage,
the dream that youth so often calls to mind?
It has gone forever; now, gray and grown old,
I have drunk life’s bitterest cup to its last drop.
Where is the dear one I have loved devotedly
through life’s changing scenes of troubled turmoil,
with the same constant, unchanging feeling,
loved at the beginning and loved to the end?

No answer comes from my silent surroundings.
Silence and gloom are always my companions.
I am as sad as if I had sunk to the ocean’s greatest depths,
where the dead of the past lie decaying.
When will this dreadful desolation end,
this suffering that holds my soul down?
Will there be no time of peaceful comfort,
no morning opening again like a rose?

Now I wander alone along life’s rough paths.
I may never again greet the friends of my youth.
Many have fallen on faraway roads
and beckon me toward the shore of death.
Beneath that low mound, where the willow weeps,
my heart’s only darling sleeps forever.
May the Angel of Death hurry with his harvest
and gather me there to dreamless rest!

Then those breaking bonds will cease forever,
and I will rest sweetly in the grave’s calm.
No storms can reach me until the morning of resurrection
calls the dead back to joy or sorrow.
No sign of danger will linger around me,
as it did when I wandered sadly and alone.
A stranger to everything except deep, dreamless peace,
I will rest quietly from the sorrows of my life.

## 10-049 — “To Victoria”: welcome to Brushy

Welcome, Victoria, to Brushy’s green slopes,
where my bachelor hopes were born and buried.

## 10-051 — “On the Banks of Brushy”: flower battles and youthful ambition

shine from far away, through the darkness between us.

in beauty, glory, and grandeur as they appeared
to my young, delighted eyes—
just as the measured rhythms of passionate verse

making it more majestic when clothed in poetry.

## 10-059 — “Apostrophe to the Brazos”

## 10-074 — Home and the ties of childhood

Home

The word “home” carries many pleasant associations. It brings the main features of our early days together in a single picture. Our first memories, companions, and relationships all spring to mind at its mention.

We remember the innocent games that filled our childhood, when we played happily among sunshine and flowers, breathing nature’s free air and spirit. We were as carefree as the brightly feathered birds singing praise in every tree, and as free from the world’s follies and vices, never imagining the troubles of later years.

## 10-109 — Varying Moments questions human systems of thought

These are moments of deep, dark bitterness,
when every higher purpose withers:
purposes that rise spark by spark
from a lone, dying flame.

Such bitter moments freeze the soul
and constrain its warm desires,
yet roll onward
like fierce, consuming fires.

which torment the mind and overwhelm the senses,
leading every trusting child astray
who does not know who steers the ship.

Humanity, you who try to lead the way

remove every burden of care,

while we wander here?

of the light given to us?
Do they not multiply with age
and shut out heaven’s light?

## 10-110 — An album as a keepsake of absent friends

formed in life’s bright, cheerful morning,

as you remember his unhappy life,

to mingle with that gently flowing tear.

## 10-117 — Siloam companions and courtship banter

## 10-131 — Oh! Let Me Forget: leaving home and the horrors of war

Let me forget the sad tears when I left
home and its attractions, which I loved so well,
and the dear friends left brokenhearted
when I spoke what I thought was my last sad farewell.
I was going to the war whose alarm rang out
a death knell for thousands in our beautiful land,
rapidly drawing from its farthest reaches
those daring heroes who would withstand the shock.
Oh, let me forget!

Let me forget the muskets’ terrible rattle,
where brothers fell fighting brothers,
and the cannon’s loud roar amid battle’s din,
portraying a miniature hell on earth.
Death rode proudly upon the wind,
fiendishly hurling darts over the plain,
or casting them fiercely again and again
over the dark waves of the swelling seas.

## 10-132 — Oh! Let Me Forget ends with grief and memory

My wild, wandering spirit toward sweet moments of rest.
The attractions surrounding her made our parting painful
and sadden the long hours of every returning day.
Too often a pearly tear starts beneath my eyelids,
staining stern manhood’s path with what the speaker calls “effeminacy.”
Oh, let me forget!

Let me forget the varied past and its pleasures,
which can never, never return in life,
with its hopes, joys, and the heart’s deepest treasures,
all now decaying in the tomb’s urn.
Like a storm-shattered tree alone on the heath,
far from its companions in the beautiful forest,
I stand looking at my branches scattered low in death
and pour my lament into the wild desert air.
Oh, let me forget!

February 1867.

## 10-133 — A portrait in verse, dated May 31, 1861

She was not very beautiful, if beauty is judged
by a classical model perfectly at rest. [[The source’s second line is incomplete or irregular.]]
She did not look bewitching, if enchantment means
having a forehead and lips transparent as the sea.
Her grace followed no prescribed rule;
her lively sparkle was never learned at school.
Her words were distinctive, like the fairies’ fabled language,
and her voice was always sweetest in the cadences of grief.
Did I say she was not beautiful? Her eyes met yours
with the softly shining purity of planetary light.
An intellectual beauty, like a light within a vase,
touched every line of her animated face with glory.
