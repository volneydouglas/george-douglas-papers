## Pages 3-3
### What the archive holds

The creator is George Douglas. The collection occupies one linear foot in two boxes and is identified as Center for Restoration Studies Manuscripts No. 5. Its inclusive and bulk dates are both given as 1830-1939. The language is English.

The collection contains photocopies of documents belonging to George Douglas, including a Civil War journal describing the battle of Sabine Pass, Texas; a Texas governor's land grant certificate; and two photographs of Douglas. It also includes copies of Nathaniel Lynn Douglas's daybook beginning in 1830, Douglas family loan and legal papers, and correspondence among family members. George Douglas served in the Confederate Army during the Civil War.

The source and date of acquisition are unknown. The accession number is R 2007.45. David Sessions and Julia Reid accessioned the collection on May 18, 2007. David Sessions, Julia Reid, and Audrey Schaffner processed it on January 29, 2014. Audrey Schaffner and Mac Ice created the finding aid on January 30, 2014; Mac Ice and Amanda Dietz updated it on January 16, 2019.

## Pages 4-4
### Access, use, and arrangement

The finding aid says the collection has been processed and is housed in ACU Special Collections and Archives at Brown Library in Abilene. Materials do not circulate. Access is available during normal business hours, with appointments preferred.

The aid states that permission to publish, quote, or reproduce items must be secured from the repository and copyright holder. Unless rights have been transferred in writing to ACU, copyright remains with the authors or their descendants under U.S. copyright law. Researchers are responsible for their use of the materials. [These are the archive's statements as printed in the 2019 finding aid, not an independent assessment of the present rights in any particular document.]

The documents are arranged numerically in seven folders, with letters, legal documents, loan papers, journals, daybooks, and other writings in no particular order within each folder. No materials are listed as separated from the collection.

The subject index begins with: Rebecca Alexander; James Bass; Emma Bell; George Bell; J. M. Bell; J. N. Bell; Sue Bell; Emily Douglas; Gail Douglas; George Douglas; Henry Douglas; Jess Douglas; Laura Douglas; Lizzie Douglas; Mary Douglas; N. L. Douglas; and Sarah Douglas.
