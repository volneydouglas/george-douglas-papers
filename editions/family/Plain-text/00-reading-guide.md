# Reading guide

This edition turns the English handwriting and older prose in the George Douglas scans into readable modern English. It is intended for family reading and comparison with a second independent reading. It is not a word-for-word transcription or a new historical account.

All 387 pages in the six primary PDFs in the iCloud George Douglas folder are accounted for, including archive covers, identifying slips, blank or damaged pages, and repeated documents. The separate 25-page transcription PDF is not included as a primary source. The finding aid describes additional archival folders, including a daybook and other correspondence, that are not among these six local PDFs; their manuscripts are outside this edition.

The four volumes contain: I, the collection guide, sermon notes, and church endorsement; II, Folder 1 correspondence and loan papers; III, Folder 2 correspondence and legal papers; and IV, Folder 3 correspondence and legal papers. Each volume can be read on its own. The PDFs are ready to share; the Word versions allow family corrections and comments.

## How to read the text

Letters retain their first-person voice. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations, and sentence structure have been modernized. A descriptive heading gives the writer, subject, and date when recoverable. Legal documents retain the principal parties, terms, amounts, dates, and recoverable land descriptions; repetitive formal language is simplified. Poems are given readable punctuation and, where necessary, a plain-English rendering.

Square brackets distinguish editorial explanations, damaged passages, and uncertain readings from the original writer's words. A name followed by a question mark is tentative. “Unclear,” “illegible,” “faded,” or “missing” means that this edition does not claim to recover the words. A smoothly written sentence is still an interpretation of handwriting, not a guarantee that every word has been read correctly. Particularly faded letters contain substantial gaps.

A later handwritten identifying slip is not treated as the original letter. Where a slip, filename, finding aid, or duplicate disagrees with the manuscript, the disagreement is recorded rather than silently resolved. Dates and names have not been standardized to fit a family tree. Original units such as varas remain in land descriptions. Money and acreage have not been converted into present-day equivalents.

Wartime news, diagnoses, accusations, and reports of deaths are the writers' accounts, often based on rumor. They are not findings independently established by this edition. The letters also contain the writers' views about slavery, race, religion, and the war. Those views belong to the documents; explanatory brackets identify context where needed.

## Finding the original

“Scan pages” always means the page count in the source PDF, including its archive cover as page 1. It does not mean a handwritten page number or the page number at the bottom of this new edition. Every document heading names its source and scan range. Entries follow scan order, except that the text within a multi-page letter is sometimes put into readable sequence; notes describe evident rearrangements.

The table of contents links to each entry in Word and PDF. PDF bookmarks and Word's Navigation pane also provide headings. Search for a date, surname, or scan range to move directly to a passage. Source filenames and coverage are listed in Volume I and in the included source ledger.

## Independence and comparison

These readings were made from the scanned manuscript images, without consulting the other agent's work. During the initial inventory, before the request for an independent version, part of the existing 25-page transcription PDF was viewed. It was then excluded and was not used further. This is therefore an independent reading in execution, but it is not a blind comparison.

Compare substantive differences against the scan: names, dates, relationships, amounts, negations, and statements about deaths matter more than wording choices in modernization. Retain both alternatives until the manuscript supports one. The most difficult passages are flagged in the text; in Folder 3, scan pages 62-67, 85-87, 100-104, 113-118 deserve particular attention. Some gaps may require a clearer photograph of the archival original.
