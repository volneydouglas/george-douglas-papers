### Jesus wept

[Scan page 3]

Of all the thoughts expressed in the language of inspiration, [damaged words] its precepts and its promises, what conveys more to our minds and hearts than the short sentence, “Jesus wept”?

Let us look for a moment at the character of Jesus. Who and what is he? If we accept the truths of the Bible, he is the Son of God, the King of the Jews, the bright and morning star! A ruler of the Jews came to Jesus and said, “We know you are a teacher come from God, for no man can do the miracles that you do unless God is with him.”

Look, then, at the power of this man. By his word, water is turned into wine. The blind receive their sight; the lame walk. By his word, the tempest is stilled, and he [walks on?] the waves of wild Galilee. By his word, the dead are called back to life again. “Truly he was the Son of God.”

But the Bible is not our only testimony to this man's greatness. Renan, in his Life of Christ, says, “All ages will proclaim that among the sons of men there were none born greater than Jesus.” His goodness, his greatness, his almighty power: we cannot doubt them. We stand in awe as we contemplate them, and our hearts acknowledge him as King of Kings and Lord of Lords.

But while we contemplate all this glorious majesty, our eyes fall on this simple sentence: “Jesus wept.” What do we learn from these two short words? We learn that he had a heart that could be touched by the sorrows and suffering of our frail humanity; that sympathy [damaged passage continues on next page]

[Scan page 4]

has the power to “make even the wretched blessed,” had its home in his breast, and that he could be touched by a feeling of our infirmities.

How our hearts thrill with love and thankfulness when we think of this blessed truth: that he sympathized with the sorrows of frail humanity.

More than eighteen hundred years have been measured by the revolving earth since Jesus walked the shores and waves of Galilee, but the influence of his words and life is still felt. Through the coming ages it will be felt until time is no more. With all the records of his wonderful life, this simple truth will pass down with the others: “Jesus wept.”

L. Douglas

Sylvanna, February 16, 1883
