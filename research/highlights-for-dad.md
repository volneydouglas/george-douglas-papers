# A few things I think Dad will enjoy from the George Douglas papers

Dad,

The best discoveries are the little details that let us hear these people speaking: looking after a baby, tending a farm, making preserves, and laughing with friends. Here are eight passages that stood out.

1. **N. L. was established in Ellis County, Texas, by 1852—and trusted to handle someone else's land.** On October 28, 1852, George A. Sublett of Rutherford County, Tennessee, authorized N. L. to sell land and collect debts for him. He called him **“my friend N L Douglas of Ellis County State of Texas”**. This gives us a firm place and date, plus a Tennessee connection. It establishes his role as an authorized agent; it does not establish that he was a lawyer. [Source: 05-025-P01, annotated volume III, PDF page 12.](https://github.com/volneydouglas/george-douglas-papers/blob/8ab7fc45fa258e203facbf9e797e5b30a572d8bb/editions/annotated/Supporting-files/Complete-transcript.txt#L2666)

2. **The estate inventory gives us a wonderfully concrete picture of the farm.** The inventory sworn on October 5, 1874, lists 640 acres on Subletts Creek, including 94 acres under cultivation, along with nine horses, twenty cattle, and fourteen hogs. My favorite entry is **“20 Bee Hives”**, valued at $40 altogether. There was also a plow, a grindstone, and a grubbing hoe: ordinary things that help us picture daily work. [Source: 05-151-P01–P03, annotated volume III, PDF page 67.](https://github.com/volneydouglas/george-douglas-papers/blob/8ab7fc45fa258e203facbf9e797e5b30a572d8bb/editions/annotated/Supporting-files/Complete-transcript.txt#L4976)

3. **Lizzie's care for Mary was formally recognized by the family.** In an October 8, 1886, deed, Mary's heirs conveyed their interests in a tract of land to Lizzie in consideration of **“Services rendered to the Said Mary Douglas during her last years of life by Lizzie Douglas”**. A legal document preserves something very personal here: Lizzie's work caring for Mary mattered enough to be recorded. [Source: 05-136-P02, annotated volume III, PDF page 56.](https://github.com/volneydouglas/george-douglas-papers/blob/8ab7fc45fa258e203facbf9e797e5b30a572d8bb/editions/annotated/Supporting-files/Complete-transcript.txt#L4589)

4. **One of the most moving letters is about taking in a baby after Gail died.** Writing to George on April 30, 1864, Lizzie reported Gail's death on the previous Saturday and said Mother had brought the little baby home. Her promise was simple: **“poor little orphan, we will all do all we can for it.”** The father was still living; “orphan” is Lizzie's wording for the child who had lost its mother. The letter does not establish the cause of Gail's death. [Source: 06-106-P01–P02, annotated volume IV, PDF page 51.](https://github.com/volneydouglas/george-douglas-papers/blob/8ab7fc45fa258e203facbf9e797e5b30a572d8bb/editions/annotated/Supporting-files/Complete-transcript.txt#L6832)

5. **The war letters describe suffering in very direct terms.** A letter signed James, written from Tupelo, Mississippi, on January 25, 1865, describes a bullet passing through his left wrist and hand the previous July. He wrote, **“I have Suffered a great deal with it”**. The letter continues with fever, hospital stays, and his wish to get home. The writing after “James” in the signature is uncertain, so this passage alone should not settle his full identity or place in the family tree. [Sources: 04-021-P03 through 04-024-P04, annotated volume II, PDF pages 15–18.](https://github.com/volneydouglas/george-douglas-papers/blob/8ab7fc45fa258e203facbf9e797e5b30a572d8bb/editions/annotated/Supporting-files/Complete-transcript.txt#L911)

6. **George's church work was a lasting part of his life.** The Sylvana congregation's endorsement, dated September 1888 in the manuscript, sent him out with **“our full permission & hearty approval.”** A later recollection by an unidentified child records his death on September 23, 1923, in Georgia and says he was brought back and buried from **“the Church that had been his life work.”** The endorsement's manuscript date differs from the finding aid's 1883 listing; the edition records that discrepancy. [Endorsement: 03-003-P01 and 03-005-P01, volume I, PDF pages 12–13.](https://github.com/volneydouglas/george-douglas-papers/blob/8ab7fc45fa258e203facbf9e797e5b30a572d8bb/editions/annotated/Supporting-files/Complete-transcript.txt#L402) [Recollection: 05-061-P01 and 05-062-P01, volume III, PDF page 26.](https://github.com/volneydouglas/george-douglas-papers/blob/8ab7fc45fa258e203facbf9e797e5b30a572d8bb/editions/annotated/Supporting-files/Complete-transcript.txt#L3245)

7. **George also sounds rather pleased with his peach preserves.** In an undated Thursday letter to Zella, he reported: **“yesterday I put up a gallon and a quart of preserves all by myself.”** He used twelve pounds of peaches and nine pounds of sugar, started at sunrise, and finished by noon. It is an affectionate glimpse of the person behind the formal papers. [Source: 04-038-P05, annotated volume II, PDF page 22.](https://github.com/volneydouglas/george-douglas-papers/blob/8ab7fc45fa258e203facbf9e797e5b30a572d8bb/editions/annotated/Supporting-files/Complete-transcript.txt#L1262)

8. **They had trouble reading family letters too—and there was plenty of laughter.** On September 3, 1923, George wrote: **“With the aid of a magnifying glass I managed to read it mostly.”** He followed that with a request to use ink or paper that would take a pencil mark. After our work deciphering the scans, that feels especially familiar. And in a letter begun August 5, 1864, Lizzie joked with a friend about washing their clothes before church: **“You ought to have heard us laughing last night.”** Those are the voices I most want us to remember. [George: 04-048-P03, volume II, PDF page 29.](https://github.com/volneydouglas/george-douglas-papers/blob/8ab7fc45fa258e203facbf9e797e5b30a572d8bb/editions/annotated/Supporting-files/Complete-transcript.txt#L1631) [Lizzie: 06-116-P01, volume IV, PDF page 57.](https://github.com/volneydouglas/george-douglas-papers/blob/8ab7fc45fa258e203facbf9e797e5b30a572d8bb/editions/annotated/Supporting-files/Complete-transcript.txt#L7010)

**The family question still open:** We have not established N. L.'s father or birthplace. The finding aid describes him as being of Murfreesboro, Tennessee, and Ellis County, Texas, but that is not proof of birth in either place. It also lists his daybook beginning January 4, 1830, in Folder 5. Those manuscript scans were not among the six supplied PDFs, making the daybook a useful next lead. [Source: 01-006-P03, annotated volume I, PDF page 8.](https://github.com/volneydouglas/george-douglas-papers/blob/8ab7fc45fa258e203facbf9e797e5b30a572d8bb/editions/annotated/Supporting-files/Complete-transcript.txt#L262)
